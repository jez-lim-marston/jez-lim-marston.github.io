  • Drag either panel to turn it; the Glycans and View buttons act on the spike, and the antibody turns on its own. Both panels are drawn at the same scale, so what you see is the real size difference. Teal shades are the three gp120/gp41 protomers, amber is the modeled Man9 glycan shield. The seven target sites are the glycans next to the V3-glycan, V2-apex, and CD4 binding-site footprints in the main report.
  • Every protein and sugar atom of the spike is drawn at its van der Waals size with a flat shade and an outline, the way Goodsell-style molecular illustrations are made. The antibody is drawn as one bead per residue.
  • The amber sugars on the antibody are its own: IgG carries a biantennary N-linked glycan on each heavy chain, in the Fc, and 1HZH includes both. They are not part of the spike. The Fab arms, which bind the target, carry no sugar in this structure.
  • The blue Fab on the spike is the antibody exactly as bound in 5FYJ, one copy per protomer (the Copies button shows all three). The spike’s sugars were modeled without any antibody present, so any sugar whose modeled position would overlap a shown Fab is hidden. In the real complex those sugars take other shapes, and PGT122 itself binds the N332 glycan, so treat the gaps as approximate.
  • The antibody in the right-hand panel is shown by itself, not docked. It is whole IgG b12, drawn only to compare size with the Fabs on the spike.
  • At this scale the glycans are not decoration. They fill the gaps between protein surface and open space, which is what an antibody has to push through.

What in this picture is data

PartStatusBasis
Env spike, proteinatomic coordinatesPDB 5FYJ, three protomers, 14,967 heavy atoms1. Height and width are measured from the coordinates.
Env spike, glycansmodeledOne random draw of the Man9 shield from the full-density model (31 sites per protomer)2,3, 11,811 atoms.
Virion size, spike countliteratureMean particle size about 145 nm (Briggs et al. 20034). Average of 14 ± 7 spikes per particle (Zhu et al.5), so the 4 and 35 buttons are not extremes of the model but the spread of the data.
Spike positionsrandomPlaced at random on the sphere with a minimum spacing. Each arrangement is one possible virion; the cut view and the outside view always show the same one.
CapsiddrawnCone about 60 nm across at the wide end, 20–30 nm at the narrow end; about 1,500 CA copies in roughly 250 hexamers and 12 pentamers6. Ticks on the wall mark the 9.6 nm hexamer spacing. Length and wall thickness are approximate.
Lipids, matrix, RNA, enzymesdrawnProcedural shapes sized to be plausible, not atomic models. Bilayer thickness is about 4.6 nm in this drawing.
Antibodyatomic coordinatesPDB 1HZH, the intact human IgG1 b12 (Saphire et al. 20017), 151.6 kDa and about 15 nm across, drawn as one bead per residue plus the Fc sugars at the same scale as the spikes, both in the virion picture and in its own panel beside the spike close-up at the same scale. It is not docked to any spike, and its placement in the virion picture is a drawn choice. The amber sugars are the Fc glycans listed in 1HZH. b12 targets the CD4 binding site; it is not one of the six antibodies in this report.
Fabs on the spikeatomic coordinatesPDB 5FYJ as crystallized: PGT122 and 35O22 as full Fab chains, VRC01 as its variable domains only8,9,10. One copy per protomer, placed with the same symmetry operations as the spike. Sugars of the modeled shield that would overlap a shown Fab are hidden, which is an approximation.

The cut removes the front half of the virion, along with any spikes that sat on it, so the picture shows only the spikes on the back half. Spike structure and glycan model come from the same work as the epitope atlas and the full-density shield in the main report.
