Research Projects

A curated list of work on how viruses and virus-derived elements influence immune cell function and dysregulation, using single-cell gene expression and computational biology, with related interests in immunology and biosecurity.

Academic Projects

scTEQuant: viral & transposable-element quantification

Bayesian quantification · single-cell & bulk RNA-seq

Postdoctoral research, Cesarman Lab, Weill Cornell Medicine

A next-generation Bayesian pipeline for locus-specific viral and transposable-element quantification from single-cell and bulk RNA-seq, developed with AI-assisted tooling and benchmarked head-to-head against established methods.

  • Locus-specific quantification at transcriptional-unit resolution across bulk, single-cell, and spatial RNA-seq
  • Improves quantification accuracy for repetitive and virus-derived loci
  • Built for direct application to EBV and HIV-1 transcriptional profiling
Key paper: Reyes-Gopar H*, Marston JL*, et al. (2025). A single-cell transposable element atlas of human cell identity. Cell Reports Methods. *Co-first authors

Viral influence on immune cells in EBV+ and EBV/HIV lymphoma

Host-pathogen immunology · viral latency & reactivation

Postdoctoral research, Cesarman Lab, Weill Cornell Medicine

Asking how viruses alter immune cell function and drive its dysregulation, by resolving EBV lytic-versus-latency programs and HIV-1 transcription alongside host gene expression at single-cell resolution across a cohort of EBV+ lymphoma patients, including EBV/HIV co-infected cases.

  • Links viral programs to changes in immune cell state and function in the tumor and its microenvironment
  • Antiviral immunology: how innate sensing and adaptive immune pressure shape EBV and HIV-1 reservoirs, and which immune states permit or suppress reactivation
  • Characterizes consequences of co-infection in immunocompromised hosts
  • Applies scTEQuant to jointly quantify host, EBV, and HIV-1 transcription

Quantification of transposable element expression in healthy and virus-infected samples

HERVs · locus-specific quantification

Doctoral research, Nixon Lab

Co-developed Stellarscope, a bioinformatic tool to quantify locus-specific expression of transposable element sequences, and published the first single-cell atlas of TE and HERV expression across the human immune system.

  • Resolves individual HERV loci rather than family-level aggregates
  • Links HERV expression patterns to specific immune cell identities and states
  • SARS-CoV-2 induces expression of specific HERV loci, even in comparison to related viruses (SARS-CoV and MERS-CoV)
  • Locus-level HERV expression can tell lymphoma subtypes apart
  • Foundation for downstream work on HERVs in immune surveillance, tolerance, and autoimmune disease

Independent Projects

Cracking the Glycan Shield

HIV-1 broadly neutralizing antibodies · glycan shield · viral escape

Independent computational project

An AI-driven pipeline that maps where six antibodies can and cannot get through HIV's glycan shield, and models how the virus escapes the ones that do. Published as a full interactive report with five companion explorers.

  • Coverage Explorer: antibody combinations checked against 525 real viruses
  • Full-Density Glycan Shield: complete Man9 glycans modeled on the 5FYJ trimer in 3D
  • Sampling Blind Spot and Origin Network: why sequencing depth hides independent escape events

Additional Interests

I also follow autoimmunity, immuno-oncology, and skin immunology, which meet my work on viral latency and endogenous retroviruses.

Autoimmunity

HERV expression & molecular mimicry

Associations between endogenous retrovirus expression and autoimmune disease, including molecular mimicry between HERV-encoded antigens and self-antigens as a possible driver of autoreactivity.

Immuno-oncology

Tumor microenvironment & viral lymphomagenesis

How EBV and HIV-1 shape the immune microenvironment of lymphoma, and how locus-level HERV expression and single-cell immune states can mark tumor subtypes and the immune responses around them.

Skin immunology

Barrier immunity & cutaneous viral latency

The skin as a site of immune surveillance and viral latency for herpesviruses, and how resident immune populations at barrier tissues differ from the systemic compartments I study in blood and lymphoid tissue.

Computational biology

Methods development & AI-assisted tooling

Broader interest in computational methods for single-cell and genomic data, and in using AI tools to accelerate development and benchmarking of new quantification approaches.
