Research.

Viruses and immune cell function

In the Cesarman Lab, I study how viruses shape immune cell function and drive its dysregulation. I resolve EBV lytic and latent programs, and HIV-1 transcription, alongside host gene expression at single-cell resolution across EBV+ lymphoma, including EBV/HIV co-infected patients. My interests also include studying endogenous retroviruses, the fossil remains of ancient infections, and how their expression in human cells affects human immune surveillance and tolerance.

Computational biology

In the Nixon Lab I built the first locus-specific single-cell atlas of transposable element and endogenous retrovirus expression across the human immune system, and showed that locus-level HERV expression can tell lymphoma subtypes apart. I co-developed Stellarscope, the locus-specific quantification tool behind that work. I now lead development of scTEQuant, a Bayesian pipeline for locus-specific viral and transposable-element quantification from single-cell and bulk RNA-seq. The scTEQuant manuscript is in preparation and its code is private until publication. Tools →

AI and biosecurity

I use AI tools in my own research every day, so I think about how they lower the technical barrier to dual-use biology and why evaluation and guardrails matter. As an independent project I mapped how six HIV antibodies get through the virus's sugar shield, the model you are looking at. Cracking the Glycan Shield →

About.

I am a physician-scientist and virologist. My research asks how viruses, and the virus-derived elements in our genomes, influence immune cell function and drive its dysregulation, using gene expression at single-cell resolution, from endogenous retroviruses in the immune system to EBV and HIV in lymphoma.

I work hands-on under BSL-2+ conditions with HIV-1, EBV, and SARS-CoV-2. That, together with daily use of AI tools, has given me a practical sense of how AI lowers the barrier to dual-use work. I believe AI can meaningfully advance biomedicine, and that doing so safely depends on people who understand both the biology and the tools.

I am interested in roles evaluating and mitigating biological risk in frontier AI systems, and more broadly in computational virology, immunology, and translational biotechnology.
